Construction Invoice Template

Progress billing by project phase, retention and change orders — a construction invoice template built for builders and subcontractors. Free in Word, Excel and PDF, or use it online — no signup.

Construction invoicing is different from ordinary billing: projects run for months, materials are bought up front, subcontractors bill the main contractor, and nobody pays the full amount in one go. A construction invoice has to tie each stage to a project phase — foundation, framing, first fix, completion — split labour from materials, list subcontractor work separately, and show retention held and change orders on their own lines. The sample below is a home extension billed exactly that way. Use the same structure for residential builds, renovations and commercial work — the structure follows the project, not the trade.

What to include on a construction invoice

  • Your business name, licence or registration number, and contact details
  • Client name and the job site address (if different from billing address)
  • Invoice number, issue date and the project phase it covers
  • A reference to the signed contract or quote number
  • Labour itemised by day or task, kept separate from materials
  • Materials with quantities — receipts attached for big-ticket items
  • Subcontractor work, listed separately from your own labour
  • Change orders, listed separately from the original scope
  • Retention held back on this stage, shown as its own deduction
  • Payments already received, and the balance remaining on the job
  • Payment terms and the due date for this stage
  • Warranty or defects liability terms for the workmanship

Why progress billing protects builders

The fastest way for a builder to bleed cash is to front months of labour and thousands in materials, then invoice once at the end. Progress billing fixes that: a deposit before you start, a progress claim as each phase is completed, and a final invoice on practical completion. This template is built for that rhythm — each stage is its own clean invoice referencing the same job and contract. Keep materials and labour on separate lines and clients (and their insurers) stop disputing totals. When a stage goes unpaid, the payment reminder generator and our guide to invoice payment terms will help you chase it professionally.

Progress billing sample: a $120,000 construction job

Construction work is rarely billed in one invoice. Progress billing splits the job into phases, and each phase gets its own invoice as the milestone is completed. Here is how a typical $120,000 build breaks down:

Many construction contracts also hold retention — often 5–10% — back from each stage until practical completion. Show retention as its own deduction line on the progress invoice, then invoice it separately once it is released, as explained in the FAQ below. Payment terms and late fees for construction

The best time to agree payment terms is before work starts, not when the invoice is overdue. Put the terms on the contract and repeat them on every progress claim, so there is never an argument about when a stage falls due. Common choices are payment on completion of each phase, or net 7, net 14 or net 30 days after the stage invoice — shorter terms protect your cash flow on jobs where you have already paid for materials, labour and subcontractors.

Invoice each stage promptly the moment its milestone is hit; a late progress claim trains clients to pay late. If a stage goes unpaid, send a written reminder right away referencing the signed-off phase — the payment reminder generator drafts escalating reminders for you.
