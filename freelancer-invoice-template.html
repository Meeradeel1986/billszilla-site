What to include on a freelancer invoice

  • Your full name or business name, email and phone
  • Client name, company and the right contact person
  • A unique invoice number and the issue date
  • Itemised services — hours × rate, or fixed fees per deliverable
  • The project or milestone each line belongs to
  • Payment terms, e.g. Net 7 or Net 14 from the invoice date
  • Your payment details — bank, IBAN or payment link
  • Any deposit already paid, shown as a deduction
  • Your tax ID and VAT/GST line, if you are registered
  • A late-payment note (fee or interest) if your contract has one

Why freelancers get paid late (and how this template helps)

Most late payments to freelancers are not malice — they are friction. The invoice went to the wrong person, the hours were not itemised so finance queried them, or the due date was never actually stated. This template attacks all three: itemised lines finance can approve at a glance, a clear due date, and payment details on the invoice itself. Pair it with short terms — Net 7 or Net 14 instead of Net 30 — and use the free time tracker to log hours as you go, then turn them into an invoice in one click. If a client still drags their feet, the payment reminder generator writes the awkward chase-up email for you.
