Your rights under the FTC Funeral Rule

The Federal Trade Commission's Funeral Rule (16 CFR Part 453, in effect since 1984) gives every family these rights:

  • Buy only what you want. No forced packages. The only charge you cannot decline is the basic services fee.
  • Prices by phone. A funeral home must give you prices if you ask, without your name or phone number first.
  • A written, itemized price list. The General Price List is yours to keep, given at the start of any in-person discussion.
  • A casket price list before you see the caskets. So you can ask about lower-priced options that are not on display.
  • An outer burial container price list before you see vaults or liners. No state law requires an outer burial container, though many cemeteries do.
  • A written statement after you decide, before you pay. Every item you selected, its price, and the total — plus an explanation of anything you are legally required to buy.
  • An alternative container for cremation. No state or local law requires a casket for cremation.
  • A casket or urn bought elsewhere must be accepted. No handling fee, no refusal, and no requirement to be present at delivery.
  • No routine embalming required by state law. Refrigeration is an acceptable alternative in most cases, and a funeral home needs your authorisation before embalming.
  • What the Rule does not cover. Cemeteries without an on-site funeral home, and third-party sellers such as casket dealers.

Nationally, the NFDA 2023 medians are $8,300 for a funeral with viewing and burial and $6,280 for a funeral with viewing and cremation. A direct cremation is $2,645 nationally when the family provides the container.

What the medians include: the basic services fee, transfer of remains, embalming and other preparation, a metal casket (burial) or an alternative container and urn (cremation), use of facilities and staff for viewing and ceremony, a hearse, a service car or van, and a basic printed memorial package.

What they do not include: the cemetery plot, opening and closing the grave, a monument or marker, flowers, the obituary, and other cash-advance items. Those are a second bill — typically $3,000–$8,000 in total.

  3. Read the range, not a single number. The planning range runs from the published Iowa average to the national median, adjusted for your choices. Take the itemized list to two or three funeral homes and compare it against their General Price Lists.

Why comparing price lists matters in Iowa

Funeral prices vary by thousands of dollars between funeral homes in the same town, and the published average is only a starting point. The FTC Funeral Rule gives you the right to an itemized price list from any funeral home — so ask two or three homes for theirs before deciding. This estimator shows the same line items the funeral home's price list will show, so you can compare like with like.
