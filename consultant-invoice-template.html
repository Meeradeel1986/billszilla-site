Day rates, workshops, reports and retainers — a consultant invoice template that corporate clients can approve without a query. Free and online.

Consultants sell expertise, and corporate clients buy it through purchase orders, accounts-payable departments and Net 30 payment runs. An invoice that gets paid on time in that world looks different from a freelancer's: it carries the client's PO number, names the engagement, breaks time down by day or session, and separates fees from expenses. What to include on a consultant invoice

  • Your firm or practice name, tax ID and contact details
  • Client company, department and your sponsor's name
  • The client's purchase order (PO) number — ask for it before you start
  • Engagement or project name matching the statement of work
  • Dates of service for each line, not just the invoice date
  • Time broken down by hours or days at the agreed rate
  • Fixed-fee deliverables (reports, workshops) as separate lines
  • Expenses itemised separately from fees, with receipts available
  • Retainer period covered, if billing a monthly retainer
  • Payment terms — Net 30 is corporate standard — and the due date

Why the PO number decides whether you get paid

In any company with an AP department, an invoice without a matching PO number goes to the bottom of the pile — or gets rejected outright. The single highest-value habit a consultant can build is getting the PO before the work starts and printing it on every invoice. BillsZilla has a dedicated PO / Reference field for exactly this. Beyond that, separate your fees from expenses, name the engagement exactly as the statement of work does, and invoice retainers in advance at the start of each month. Do those three things and the payment terms you agreed actually hold.
