What to include on a designer invoice

  • Your studio name and contact details
  • Client name and the project name or brand
  • What the fee covers — concepts, formats, deliverables
  • How many revision rounds are included, and the rate for extras
  • Each deliverable as its own line (logo, guidelines, templates)
  • Licensing: what usage rights transfer, and what the buyout costs
  • When source files are handed over (after final payment is standard)
  • Deposit already paid — commonly 50% for design work
  • Payment terms and the final-payment due date
  • File formats you will supply (SVG, PDF, PNG…)

Why itemising design work ends revision creep

“Just one more tweak” is the most expensive sentence in design. When your invoice template prints the included revision rounds and the per-round rate — as the sample does — clients self-edit their feedback and happily pay for genuine extras. The same logic applies to licensing: listing the source-file buyout as a priced line turns an awkward ask into a simple purchase. Protect the final files too: standard practice is to release print-ready and source files only after the final payment clears.
