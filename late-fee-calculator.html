Why a clear late-fee rule gets you paid

A late fee is not really about the money — it is about the deadline. When an invoice quietly sits unpaid, there is no cost to the client for waiting. A stated rule changes that: 1.5% a month, or a flat fee after 30 days, turns “pay whenever” into a date with a price. The trick is stating the rule before the work starts, in the payment terms on the invoice itself, and then applying it consistently. This calculator gives you the exact number to quote, whether your rule is monthly, annual or flat. Once you have it, add the fee as its own line on the invoice with the free invoice generator, and follow up with the payment reminder generator — a polite reminder with a precise total lands very differently from a vague “just checking in”.

Late fee calculator FAQ

Multiply the overdue amount by the monthly rate, then by the days overdue divided by 30. A $1,000 invoice at 1.5% per month, 60 days overdue, is 1,000 × 1.5% × 60 ÷ 30 = $30.00 in late fees, for a $1,030.00 total due.
Multiply the overdue amount by the annual rate, then by the days overdue divided by 365. A $1,000 invoice at 12% per year, 60 days overdue, is 1,000 × 12% × 60 ÷ 365 = $19.73 in late fees, for a $1,019.73 total due.
