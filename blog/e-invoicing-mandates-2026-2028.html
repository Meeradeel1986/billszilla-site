E-Invoicing Is Going Mandatory: The 2026–2028 Deadlines You Need to Know

Spain just locked in its dates, France is already live, and more countries follow every year. If you sell to clients abroad, this wave will reach you sooner than you think.

For years, e-invoicing has been one of those things that was always "coming soon." In October 2026, it stopped being a prediction. Spain published the order that fixes its mandatory B2B e-invoicing calendar, France's reform is already live, and Germany, the UAE and Ireland all have dates on the books. The direction of travel is now unmistakable: invoices are becoming structured data, not documents you email.

If you are a freelancer or a small business with clients in other countries, this matters even if none of these rules apply to you directly today. Here is what happened, the deadlines that count, and what to do about them.

What happened in Spain

On 6 October 2026, Orden HAC/1028/2026 took effect in Spain. The order sets up the country's public e-invoicing solution and, with it, the start dates for mandatory business-to-business e-invoicing:

  • 6 October 2027 — businesses and professionals with turnover above €8 million in the previous year must start issuing e-invoices.
  • 6 October 2028 — every other business and professional follows.

The mandate covers invoices between businesses and professionals established or resident in Spain. Sales to consumers are outside it, and ordinary simplified invoices are excluded too.

Spain's system works through private e-invoicing platforms or a free public solution run by the tax agency (AEAT). Businesses that do not use the public solution must send the agency a faithful copy of each invoice in UBL format, and buyers must report whether they accepted or rejected an invoice — and when they paid it — within four calendar days. This is separate from VERI*FACTU, Spain's rule on tamper-proof invoicing software, which the finance ministry has indicated may move to October 2028; that change still needs legislation, so treat it as expected rather than final.

The worldwide deadlines at a glance

Spain is the latest trigger, but it is one piece of a much bigger wave. Here are the dates that matter most:

France went first among this group: since 1 September 2026, every business established in France and subject to VAT must be able to receive e-invoices, with issuing already mandatory for larger companies. Germany has required businesses to be able to receive e-invoices since January 2025; issuing becomes the obligation from 2027. The UAE runs its mandate through accredited service providers on the Peppol network, and Ireland's Finance (No. 2) Bill 2026, published in October 2026, would put its phased rollout into law — though the bill is still going through the legislative process.

What this means for freelancers and small businesses

First, the single most important distinction: a PDF emailed to a client is not a structured e-invoice. A PDF is a document a person can read. A structured e-invoice is data — in formats like UBL, XRechnung or Factur-X — that accounting software can read, validate and process automatically. Hybrid formats such as ZUGFeRD and Factur-X look like PDFs but carry the machine-readable data inside, which is what makes them count.

Second, the mandates are generally built around where a business is established, not merely where it holds a VAT number. A freelancer registered for VAT in Germany, France or Spain without a fixed establishment there is typically outside the issuing obligation — but that is not the end of the story. Business customers in those countries will increasingly ask their suppliers for structured invoices once their own systems run on them, and local suppliers will start sending structured files that you need to be able to read and store.

Third, consumer sales are mostly outside these mandates. If you only sell to individuals, the direct impact is small. But the moment you invoice a company abroad — a design client in Berlin, a consultancy customer in Paris, a distributor in Dubai — you are in the world these rules are reshaping.

The invoice itself is not changing. The mandatory details — who, what, how much, what tax — stay the same. What changes is the format it must travel in, and the speed at which tax authorities can see it.

What to do now

  1. Map your clients. List the countries your business customers are in and check whether any of the dates above touch you — directly or through a customer who will ask you for a structured invoice.
  2. Keep your invoice details perfect. Structured invoicing punishes sloppy data. Correct client names, tax numbers, line items and tax calculations are the foundation everything else sits on.
  3. Ask your biggest B2B clients what they will need, and by when.
  4. Make sure you can receive and store structured files. If a supplier sends you an XRechnung or UBL file, you need to be able to open it and keep it in its original format.
  5. Watch the Irish bill and the EU ViDA timeline. Ireland's draft legislation and the 2030 EU-wide deadline will set the next wave of dates.

None of this means you need expensive software tomorrow. It means the humble invoice is becoming a piece of digital infrastructure — and the freelancers who keep clean, correct invoicing habits will have the easiest transition.
