How to Save and Send an Invoice as a PDF

Why PDF is the format clients prefer — and how to save, name, send and store your invoices properly.

You've done the work and filled in the invoice. One step remains: getting it to your client in a format that arrives looking exactly as you intended. That format is PDF. Here's why, and how to handle the whole save-and-send routine like a professional.

Why send your invoice as a PDF?

It looks identical everywhere. A PDF locks your layout, fonts and logo in place. Your client sees the same clean invoice on a phone, a laptop or an office desktop — a spreadsheet or word-processing file, by contrast, can reflow, shift columns or substitute fonts on a different machine.

It can't be casually edited. Anyone can open a spreadsheet and quietly change a number. A PDF isn't impossible to alter, but it can't be tweaked by accident or with a stray keystroke — the totals you sent are the totals your client reads.

It prints cleanly. Many clients still print invoices for their files. A PDF prints the same on any printer and any paper size, with nothing cut off at the edges.

It opens on anything. Every modern device opens PDFs natively, no paid software required. Accounts departments around the world are set up to receive them — it's the format their systems expect.

How to save an invoice as a PDF

If you make your invoice with BillsZilla's free invoice generator, the PDF is built right in your browser:

2. Pick a template and check the preview looks right.
3. Click Download/Print ▾ and save the PDF. The file is generated on your device — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Made your invoice in a spreadsheet or document instead? Open the print dialog (Ctrl+P on Windows, Cmd+P on Mac) and choose "Save as PDF" as the printer. Check the preview before saving — you want the whole invoice on the page, with the total visible and nothing spilling onto a second sheet.

Name the file like a professional

"invoice.pdf" tells your client nothing, and by the tenth one, neither of you can tell them apart. Use a consistent pattern that includes the invoice number and your business name, such as Invoice-INV-0042-YourBusiness.pdf. Your client can file it instantly, their accountant can match it to a payment, and you can find it months later in seconds. The same pattern helps you, too — a folder of uniformly named invoices is its own index.

Send it the right way

By email: attach the PDF itself — never a screenshot or a photo of the invoice. Put the essentials in the subject line so it survives a crowded inbox: "Invoice INV-0042 from YourBusiness — $850, due 7 November." In the body, keep it to a few friendly lines: what the invoice is for, the amount, the due date, and how to pay. Send it to the person who actually handles payments, which isn't always the person you did the work for.

By WhatsApp or chat: attach the PDF as a document, not as an image. Sending it as a photo compresses it into a blurry picture your client can't print or forward properly; sending it as a document keeps the real file intact. Add one short message with the amount and due date so the important details are visible without opening anything.

Either way, keep a copy of what you sent. The exact PDF your client received is your proof if a total is ever questioned.

Need to merge several files, or shrink a big PDF?

Sometimes one invoice travels with attachments: a signed quote, timesheets, receipts for expenses. Clients prefer a single file over five separate ones. If you need to merge several PDFs into one — or a scan has made your PDF too large to email and you need to compress it first — PDFZippy offers free tools for that which run entirely in your browser, so your documents are never uploaded to a server. Combine or shrink the file, then send it as above.

Keep your copies organised

Saving the PDF is only half the habit — storing it well is the other half. Keep one folder for invoices, split by year (or by client, if you have a few large ones), and file every invoice the day you send it, using the same naming pattern. Back the folder up: a cloud drive or a second device means a lost laptop doesn't mean lost records. Most businesses are expected to keep financial records for several years, so treat your sent invoices as documents worth keeping, not files worth deleting. When your future self — or your accountant — goes looking, everything will be exactly where it should be.

The short version

Write the invoice, save it as a PDF, name it with its number and your business name, attach it as a real file, and file your copy the same day.
